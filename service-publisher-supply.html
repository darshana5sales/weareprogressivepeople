Supply-side & publisher development recruitment

Supply is a relationship business running on a technical stack. The leadership has to be fluent in both.

Supply partnerships leadership is the least well-understood senior discipline in AdTech, and one of the hardest to hire. The role sits between a publisher's commercial team and a platform's engineering roadmap, and it is judged on relationships that take years to build and a quarter to lose.

A Supply Director is not a salesperson with a different title. They are accountable for the quality, exclusivity and durability of the inventory a platform can offer — which means negotiating commercial terms, defending take rate, shaping the integration roadmap and holding credibility with a publisher's ad operations team at the same time.

We search for that combination explicitly: commercial standing with genuine technical fluency in ad serving, header bidding, supply-path optimisation, curation and identity. We work retained and contingent, matched to the brief.

Supply leadership at SSPs, exchanges and platforms

Supply-path optimisation has permanently changed what a supply leader is hired to do. When buyers consolidated the number of paths they would transact through, the supply-side conversation stopped being about breadth of access and started being about being the path a publisher and a buyer both choose.

The senior leaders who navigate that well can hold two arguments at once: the commercial case for a publisher to prioritise their integration, and the technical case for why that path carries less latency, better match rates and cleaner signal. Hiring for one half of that and hoping the other develops is the most common failure mode we see in supply searches.

We market-map the supply leadership community directly. It is a small, well-networked group, and the strongest candidates are almost never on the market — which is precisely why a structured, directly-approached search outperforms an advertised role in this discipline.

Publisher development and partnerships

Publisher development leadership owns the front end of that relationship: identifying the inventory a platform needs, winning the integration, and then growing the share of a publisher's monetisation that runs through it.

The remit increasingly extends past classic display supply into CTV and BVOD inventory, curation and packaged audience deals, retail media networks bringing their own inventory to market, and the commerce and identity partnerships that sit alongside them. We recruit across all of it.

How we qualify supply candidates

Our founders have sat on both sides of these conversations — inside media owners selling the inventory, and alongside the platforms buying access to it. That is what a structured supply qualification looks like in practice:

  • Relationship depth, tested. Not which publishers they have worked with, but who specifically would take their call today, and what the last commercially difficult conversation with that account was.
  • Technical fluency, tested. Header bidding mechanics, SPO and reseller dynamics, ads.txt and sellers.json hygiene, curation, and how identity solutions actually perform on their inventory.
  • Commercial judgement, tested. How they have defended take rate under pressure, and what they have walked away from.
  • Reference triangulation. We reference beyond the names offered, including on the publisher side of past relationships.
