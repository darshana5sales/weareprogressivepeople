What a real Head of Programmatic looks like in 2026.

By Keith Rattray 19 February 2026 7 min read
On this page

  • On the supply side
  • The CTV dimension
  • How we qualify programmatic leadership
  • Briefing the role well

Ten years ago, a Head of Programmatic was largely an execution leader: a person who understood the platforms, could run a trading team, and could be trusted with a budget. The title survived. The job did not.

Briefs still frequently describe the 2016 version of the role, which is one reason programmatic searches stall — the market has moved and the job description has not.

On the buy side: from execution to accountability

Agency and advertiser-side programmatic leadership is now judged on outcomes that used to sit elsewhere. The remit routinely includes:

  • Supply-path strategy. Deciding which paths the business transacts through, defending those decisions to clients, and being able to show the working.
  • Curation and inventory quality. Curated marketplaces and packaged audience deals have shifted the conversation from reach to composition.
  • Measurement credibility. With signal loss now permanent rather than pending, the leader has to be fluent in incrementality, MMM and clean-room measurement — and honest about their limits.
  • Client-facing commercial responsibility. The role increasingly sits in front of clients, defending fee structures and transparency arrangements.

The strongest buy-side candidates are as comfortable in a commercial review as in a trading desk. Briefs that screen only for platform expertise systematically miss them.

On the supply side: yield is a commercial job now

Publisher-side programmatic leadership has changed in a different direction. The Head of Programmatic or Director of Yield at a publisher or broadcaster is now responsible for how the entire inventory pool is monetised, not just the open exchange remainder.

That means owning the relationship between direct sales and programmatic rather than treating them as rivals, running curation and packaged deals with buyers, holding a sensible position on identity solutions, and defending the technical hygiene — ads.txt, sellers.json, supply-path efficiency — that determines whether buyers prioritise the path at all.

The publisher-side programmatic leader is now accountable for the commercial architecture of the whole inventory pool, not the remainder of it.

The CTV dimension

Almost every senior programmatic brief now carries a CTV component, and this is where most market maps go wrong. Programmatic CTV is not display programmatic with bigger creative. The supply is scarcer, the buying is more relationship-led, frequency management across walled gardens is genuinely hard, and measurement is a different discipline.

A candidate with ten years of excellent display programmatic experience and no CTV exposure is not automatically the wrong hire — but the brief needs to say whether CTV fluency is required on day one or developable, because that single decision changes the shortlist entirely.

How we qualify programmatic leadership

The useful questions are specific and operational:

  1. Walk me through a supply-path decision you made that reduced the number of partners, and how you defended it internally.
  2. What is actually in your curated deals, and how do you price them against open exchange?
  3. How do you handle frequency across CTV and open web when the identifiers do not join?
  4. Where does your programmatic revenue cannibalise direct, and how do you manage that conversation with the sales team?
  5. Which measurement claims made to your clients or buyers would you not personally defend?

The last question is the most revealing. Candidates who answer it honestly tend to be the ones who will tell a CEO something inconvenient in month three.

Briefing the role well

Three decisions make a programmatic search straightforward rather than protracted: whether the role is buy side or supply side in its centre of gravity, whether CTV fluency is required or developable, and whether the leader owns a number or supports one. Briefs that leave all three open produce longlists nobody is happy with.

We run programmatic, trading and AdOps leadership search across buy and supply side. If you are shaping a role and would like a market view first, brief our team.
