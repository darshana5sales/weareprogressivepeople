Executive Search

Why boutique headhunters outperform global firms on senior AdTech roles.

By Keith Rattray 22 January 2026 7 min read
On this page
  • Off-limits is the biggest hidden variable in search
  • Who actually does the work
  • Domain fluency is existential for a specialist
  • Fewer gates, faster process
  • Where the global firm is the right answer
  • How to test the claim rather than take it

This is a self-interested argument and worth reading as one. We are a boutique. But the reasons a boutique tends to outperform a global generalist on a senior AdTech mandate are structural rather than cultural, and they are testable — which makes them worth setting out properly, including the cases where they do not hold.

1. Off-limits is the biggest hidden variable in search

Every search firm agrees not to approach employees of its clients for a period. That is reasonable. The consequence is that the larger the client list, the larger the population a firm cannot touch.

In a broad market this barely matters. In senior AdTech it is decisive. For a specialist CRO or Head of Supply mandate there may be twenty-five genuinely qualified people. If eight work at businesses a global firm serves elsewhere, a third of the market is unavailable before the search starts — and you will not be told which third.

A boutique with a deliberately small client list carries a short, disclosable off-limits list. Ask any firm you are evaluating to tell you theirs, in writing, before you engage. The quality of the answer is informative regardless of firm size.

Ask any search firm to put its current off-limits list in writing before you engage. The quality of the answer tells you most of what you need to know.

2. Who actually does the work

At a large firm, the model is leveraged by design: a partner sells, a principal manages, and an associate or research team does the mapping and first-round qualification. It is an efficient way to run many mandates and it works well in markets where the assessment is fairly standardised.

Senior AdTech is not such a market. The qualification conversation requires someone who can hold a credible discussion about supply-path economics or CTV measurement with a candidate who does it for a living. A researcher two years out of university, working from a competency framework, cannot — and senior candidates notice within five minutes, which affects whether they take the role seriously at all.

At a boutique, the person you brief is the person who makes the approach. That is the entire operating model, and it is why boutiques cap the number of live mandates they take.

3. Domain fluency is existential for a specialist

A global firm can afford a weak AdTech practice; the other thirty practices carry it. A boutique specialising in advanced media cannot afford a single conversation in which it is out of its depth, because the market is small and word travels.

That asymmetry is why specialist firms tend to know the market better. It is not virtue, it is survival.

4. Fewer gates, faster process

Boutique processes tend to complete faster for unglamorous reasons: fewer handoffs, no internal committee reviewing the longlist, no scheduling through three offices, and a partner who can make a decision on the phone. On a senior commercial mandate, weeks saved in process are weeks of revenue.

Where the global firm is the right answer

Three cases, and they are real:

  • Genuinely simultaneous multi-market build-outs — five leadership hires across five countries at once, with local employment complexity attached.
  • Board and Chair appointments where an investor or plc governance process expects a particular firm's process and brand.
  • Markets where the boutique has no network. An honest specialist will tell you when this is the case, and a good one will make an introduction rather than take the mandate anyway.

How to test the claim rather than take it

Whichever type of firm you are considering, four questions settle it:

  1. Name the senior roles in this discipline you have closed in the past twelve months.
  2. Who personally runs my mandate, and what else are they running concurrently?
  3. What is on your off-limits list today that touches this search?
  4. Talk to me for ten minutes about what has changed in this market in the last year.

The fourth question is the one that cannot be prepared for, and it is the one we would weight most heavily.

If you would like to test ours, brief our team — we are happy to have that conversation before any commercial discussion.
