Where UK, EMEA and global leadership remits now overlap.

By Scott Deutrom 15 January 2026 8 min read
On this page
  • Why scope expanded without headcount following
  • What this does to a brief
  • Assessing genuine regional capability
  • Where the search practice sits
  • A note on compensation

One of the more consequential quiet shifts in advanced media hiring is that the scope of senior roles has grown while the location of the people filling them has not. A commercial leader who would once have owned the UK now frequently owns UK and EMEA, sometimes with global product or partnership responsibility attached, while remaining based in London.

That has real implications for how roles are briefed, priced and assessed — and it is worth separating from the question of where a business or its search partner is based.

Why scope expanded without headcount following

Several things happened at once. Platform businesses consolidated regional structures after the funding environment tightened, replacing country leadership layers with a smaller number of broader roles. Remote and hybrid working made a regional remit run from one city genuinely workable in a way it was not in 2018. And the buy side consolidated in parallel — when agency groups centralise investment decisions regionally, the sell side follows.

The result is a market where “Sales Director” may mean one market or five, and where the title alone tells you almost nothing about the job.

What this does to a brief

The most common failure we see is a brief that carries a regional scope and a single-market compensation benchmark. Those roles do not close. A leader who has run EMEA revenue is priced against other EMEA roles, not against the UK equivalent of their title.

The second most common is scope that is never actually specified. “International” in a job description can mean anything from occasional travel to owning P&L in nine countries. Candidates read the ambiguity as risk — usually the risk of a job that expands after they sign.

Good briefs for these roles state four things explicitly: which markets are in scope on day one, which are expected within eighteen months, whether the leader has local people or is the local presence, and who owns the number in each market.

Assessing genuine regional capability

Regional experience is easy to claim and harder to evidence. The questions that work are practical rather than conceptual:

  1. Which markets did you personally carry a number in, and which did you oversee through someone else?
  2. Where did your regional model break, and what did you change?
  3. How did you handle a market where the local buying behaviour did not match the regional playbook?
  4. What did you stop doing when the scope expanded? — the most useful question of the four.

The strongest regional leaders answer the last one immediately, because managing scope expansion is mostly about deciding what to drop. Candidates who cannot answer it have usually held a regional title rather than a regional job.

Where the search practice sits, and why it is a separate question

It is worth stating plainly, because the two things are routinely conflated: a UK-based practice and a global remit are not in tension.

Progressive People is a UK-based executive search practice. The leaders we place are UK-based and, less often, EMEA-based. What reaches further is the scope of their roles — single-market, regional and global — and the places those appointments have landed, including New York, Singapore, Paris, Amsterdam, Berlin and Madrid.

Claiming an operating footprint we do not have would not help a client, because the value in this market is network depth rather than office count. Being straight about where that depth sits is more useful than a map.

A note on compensation

Regional roles based in the UK sit in an awkward benchmarking position: above the UK market rate for the title, usually below the US equivalent, and complicated further by equity where the business is US-headquartered. The practical advice is to benchmark against the scope rather than the title, agree the range before approaching anyone, and be clear internally about what happens if the right candidate sits above it. That conversation is much cheaper at brief stage than at offer stage.

If you are shaping a role with regional or global scope and would like a benchmark view first, brief our team.
