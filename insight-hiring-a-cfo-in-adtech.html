AdTech Hiring

Hiring a CFO for an AdTech business.

By Keith Rattray 8 January 2026 6 min read
On this page

  • Take-rate economics and cost of revenue
  • Match the CFO to the stage
  • The commercial partnership test
  • Briefing the search

An AdTech CFO search sits in an awkward gap. Generalist finance search firms know CFOs but not the sector, and are prone to presenting excellent candidates who have never encountered the specific accounting problems an AdTech business creates. Sector specialists know the market but often lack finance search depth.

The gap matters because AdTech businesses carry finance complexity that most technology businesses of equivalent size do not.

1. Gross versus net is not a technicality

The single most important sector-specific competence for an AdTech CFO is the revenue recognition question: whether the business reports gross media spend or net revenue, and whether that treatment is defensible.

It sounds like an accounting footnote. It is not. It determines the headline revenue number, the growth rate investors see, the margin profile, the comparability to peers, and — in diligence — whether a valuation survives contact with an acquirer's accountants. Businesses have had exits repriced over exactly this.

A candidate who has genuinely worked through a gross-to-net restatement, or defended a principal-versus-agent position in an audit, brings something no generic CFO experience substitutes for.

Gross versus net sounds like an accounting footnote. It determines the growth rate investors see and whether a valuation survives diligence.

2. Take-rate economics and cost of revenue

An AdTech CFO has to be fluent in where margin actually comes from and how fragile it is: take rate and the pressure supply-path optimisation puts on it, cloud and infrastructure costs that scale with query volume rather than revenue, data and identity licensing costs, and the working-capital problem of paying publishers before agencies pay you.

That last point is chronically underweighted in briefs. Several otherwise healthy AdTech businesses have run into difficulty on payment timing rather than profitability, and a CFO who has managed that cycle is worth a great deal.

3. Match the CFO to the stage, honestly

The finance leader who builds reporting discipline in a Series A business is rarely the one who runs a sale process, and vice versa. Broadly:

  • Growth-stage: systems, reporting credibility, unit economics that survive an investor's spreadsheet, and the first proper board pack.
  • Scale-up: multi-entity and multi-currency complexity, revenue recognition at scale, and building a finance team rather than being one.
  • Pre-exit: diligence readiness, restating history defensibly, and sitting credibly opposite an acquirer's advisers.

Briefs that ask for all three get a longlist of people who have done one of them and claim the others.

4. The commercial partnership test

In a business where pricing, take rate and deal structure determine survival, the CFO cannot be purely a controller. The strongest AdTech CFOs sit genuinely alongside the CRO — pressure-testing deal economics before they are signed rather than reporting on them afterwards.

A useful interview question: describe a deal you stopped, and how that conversation went with the commercial team. Candidates who have never stopped one have either had an unusually disciplined sales organisation or have not been in the room.

Briefing the search

A good AdTech CFO brief specifies the stage and the next eighteen months honestly — including whether a raise or a sale is contemplated; states the current revenue recognition treatment and any known exposure; is clear about whether the hire builds a team or is the team; and sets a compensation envelope that accounts for equity being a meaningful part of the package at this level.
