Privacy Policy.

Last updated 2 November 2026 Version 1.1

This policy explains how Progressive People collects, uses, shares and protects personal data, and what your rights are. It applies to candidates, clients, prospective clients, newsletter subscribers and anyone who visits this website.

1. Who we are

Progressive People is a UK-based executive search and recruitment practice. For the purposes of the UK General Data Protection Regulation (UK GDPR) and the Data Protection Act 2018, Progressive People is the data controller for the personal data described in this policy.

We are a small practice and do not have a statutory obligation to appoint a Data Protection Officer. Data protection questions are handled by the founders directly.

2. The personal data we collect

What we hold depends on your relationship with us.

Candidates and prospective candidates

  • Name, contact details and location.
  • CV, career history, qualifications, skills and professional achievements.
  • Current and expected compensation, notice period and availability.
  • Interview notes, assessment records and our own commentary on suitability.
  • References and the information referees provide about you.
  • Right-to-work information where an appointment progresses.
  • Publicly available professional information, including LinkedIn profiles and published company information.

Clients and prospective clients

  • Name, job title, business contact details and the organisation you represent.
  • Records of briefings, correspondence, proposals and engagement terms.
  • Billing and payment information.

Website visitors and subscribers

  • Information you submit through our contact or CV forms.
  • Your email address if you subscribe to our insights newsletter.
  • Your name, email address and any notes you add if you book an intro call through our booking calendar, which is provided by Calendly.
  • Technical information generated by your visit, as described in our Cookie Policy.

We do not seek to collect special category data (such as health, ethnicity or religious belief) and ask that you do not include it in a CV or enquiry. Where it is unavoidable in the course of an appointment — for example a reasonable-adjustment request during an interview process — we will handle it only with your explicit consent or where the law otherwise permits.

3. How we collect it

  • Directly from you — when you contact us, submit a CV, subscribe to the newsletter, book a call, or speak to us about a mandate or a role.
  • From public and professional sources — LinkedIn and other professional networks, company websites, published announcements and industry press. Direct research of this kind is fundamental to how executive search works.
  • From referrals — introductions and recommendations from people in our network. If someone refers you to us, we will tell you who, when we first make contact.
  • From referees — where you have progressed in a process and referencing has been agreed with you.

4. Why we use it, and our lawful basis

Under the UK GDPR we must have a lawful basis for each use of personal data. Ours are set out below.

What we doLawful basis
Identify, approach and assess candidates for a search, and present suitable candidates to a client. Legitimate interests — running an executive search practice, and connecting senior professionals with relevant opportunities.
Maintain a candidate network so we can contact you about future roles. Legitimate interests, and consent where you have asked us to keep you on file.
Take up references and verify right to work where an appointment is progressing. Legitimate interests and, for right to work, legal obligation.
Deliver a search mandate for a client and administer the engagement. Contract, or legitimate interests where our contract is with the organisation rather than with you personally.
Send our insights newsletter. Consent, which you can withdraw at any time.
Send occasional relevant commentary to existing business contacts. Legitimate interests, subject to your right to object at any time.
Keep accounting records and meet tax, audit and regulatory obligations. Legal obligation.
Establish, exercise or defend legal claims. Legitimate interests.

Where we rely on legitimate interests, we have considered whether our interest is outweighed by your rights and freedoms. Direct approaches to senior professionals about relevant, seniority-appropriate opportunities are a well-established and expected feature of the executive search market. You can object to this processing at any time, and we will stop unless we have compelling grounds to continue.

5. Who we share it with

  • Clients. Where you are a candidate on a live mandate, we share your CV and relevant assessment information with the hiring organisation. We will tell you which organisation before we do so, and we do not submit your details to a client without your agreement.
  • Referees you have nominated, and only after you have agreed to referencing.
  • Service providers who process data on our behalf under written terms — for example email hosting, our website host, our call-booking provider (Calendly), a customer relationship or applicant tracking system, and our accountants. They act on our instructions and may not use your data for their own purposes.
  • Professional advisers, including lawyers and auditors, where necessary.
  • Regulators, law enforcement or other authorities where we are legally required to do so.

We do not sell personal data, and we do not share it for third-party marketing.

6. International transfers

We are a UK-based practice and our data is primarily held in the UK and the European Economic Area. Where a search carries a regional or global remit, or where a service provider stores data outside the UK, personal data may be transferred internationally. Where that happens we rely on UK adequacy regulations, or on the International Data Transfer Agreement or the UK Addendum to the EU Standard Contractual Clauses, together with any additional safeguards the transfer requires.

8. Your rights

Under the UK GDPR you have the right to:

  • Be informed about how we use your data — which is what this policy is for.
  • Access a copy of the personal data we hold about you.
  • Rectification of data that is inaccurate or incomplete.
  • Erasure of your data where we no longer have a good reason to keep it.
  • Restrict processing in certain circumstances.
  • Data portability for data you provided to us, where processing is based on consent or contract.
  • Object to processing based on legitimate interests, and to object to direct marketing at any time.
  • Withdraw consent at any time where consent is the basis we rely on.

We do not make decisions about candidates by automated means alone. Every shortlist decision at Progressive People is made by a person.

To exercise any of these rights, email scott@weareprogressivepeople.com. We will respond within one month. There is no charge, unless a request is manifestly unfounded or excessive.

9. Complaints

If you are unhappy with how we have handled your personal data, please tell us first so we can put it right. You also have the right to complain to the UK supervisory authority, the Information Commissioner’s Office, at ico.org.uk/make-a-complaint or on 0303 123 1113.

10. Newsletter and marketing

Our insights newsletter is sent only to people who have asked for it. Every email includes a one-click unsubscribe link, and we act on unsubscribes immediately. We do not pass subscriber details to anyone else.

11. Cookies

Our use of cookies and similar technologies is covered separately in our Cookie Policy.

12. Security

We use access controls, encrypted transmission and reputable, contractually bound service providers to protect personal data. No transmission over the internet can be guaranteed completely secure, but we take the protection of candidate and client information seriously, and a material proportion of our work is confidential by design.

13. Changes to this policy

We may update this policy as our practice or the law changes. The version number and date at the top of the page show when it was last revised. Material changes will be flagged on this page.
