Executive Search

How to choose the right AdTech executive search firm in 2026.

By Scott Deutrom 12 March 2026 8 min read
On this page

  • Boutique vs. global
  • Retained vs. contingent
  • The domain fluency test
  • Questions to ask in the pitch

The AdTech leadership market in 2026 is materially harder to hire into than it was three years ago. Identity has fragmented, supply-path optimisation has compressed margin assumptions across the entire programmatic stack, CTV has matured into a discipline with its own commercial gravity, and the senior commercial leaders who actually understand the new shape of the industry are scarce, well-compensated and rarely on the market.

For founders, boards and PE-backed leadership teams about to commission a senior AdTech, programmatic or advanced TV search, the choice of executive search firm is therefore the single highest-leverage decision in the process. The right partner will close a placement in twelve weeks. The wrong one will burn nine months and end with a hire that does not last the bonus cycle.

This article is a practical framework for making that decision.

Why this matters more than it used to

Three structural shifts in the advanced media industry have meaningfully changed what a senior executive search firm needs to be able to do.

First, the relevant candidate pool has narrowed. Genuine experience running a programmatic business through cookie deprecation and supply-path optimisation does not sit in many people. The same is true of advanced TV commercial leadership that has navigated both linear and CTV at scale, or AdTech CRO experience that combines SaaS go-to-market discipline with media industry context.

Second, off-limits has expanded. The bigger the search firm, the longer the off-limits list. For senior AdTech roles, where there might be twenty genuinely qualified candidates, an off-limits list that excludes eight of them is a real problem.

Third, the brief itself has become harder to write. The right CRO for an AdTech business in 2026 looks meaningfully different from the right CRO in 2021. A search partner without genuine, current domain fluency cannot help refine the brief — and an unrefined brief is the most common reason senior searches fail.

Boutique vs. global: it is not actually that close

For senior AdTech and advanced media mandates, a boutique executive search firm will typically outperform a global generalist. The reasons are structural, not marketing-led:

  • Off-limits is shorter. A boutique with a small client list cannot retain a candidate they could have approached for a different client.
  • Founder leverage is higher. At a boutique, the person you brief is the person who runs the search. At a global firm, the partner you meet at the pitch is rarely the person doing the qualification work.
  • Domain fluency is higher. Boutiques live or die on specialism. A boutique AdTech search firm cannot afford to be vague about supply-path optimisation.
  • Speed is higher. Fewer mandates, fewer handoffs, fewer process gates.

The case for a global firm is narrower than it used to be, and limited to two scenarios: simultaneous multi-territory leadership rollouts that genuinely require local presence in five regions, and certain board-level or Chair searches where the global firm's brand stamp carries investor signalling weight. For almost everything else, a boutique search firm is the structurally better choice.

Retained vs. contingent: match the model to the brief

The honest answer is that neither model is universally right, and a search partner who insists otherwise is selling rather than advising.

Retained search is the correct model when the brief demands genuine market mapping, direct outreach to passive candidates, confidentiality, or the replacement of an incumbent. The staged fee is what funds work that contingent recruitment cannot economically justify: research, direct approach, structured competency interviews, reference triangulation and a guided offer process. Almost every board and C-suite appointment falls here.

Contingent recruitment is a different product, and it is not a lesser one when applied correctly. For a well-defined management hire in a liquid part of the market — where qualified candidates are both numerous and reachable — it can be faster and better value.

The signal to look for is whether a search partner will tell you which of those two your brief actually is, including when the answer costs them fee. We cover this in more depth in retained vs. contingent headhunting.

The domain fluency test

The single most useful diagnostic for evaluating an executive search firm is a conversation about the actual market — not about their process, their methodology, or their previous placements. Ask the partner you would be working with to talk about:

  1. The current state of supply-path optimisation and what it means for SSP-side CRO mandates.
  2. How the addressable TV market is structurally different from the broader CTV market.
  3. What a credible Head of Programmatic at a major UK publisher actually does day-to-day in 2026.
  4. The compensation expectation for a Tier 1 SaaS-AdTech CRO carrying a UK and EMEA number.

A partner who can answer all four with the same fluency as a CRO is the partner you want running your search. A partner who cannot is, by definition, going to need your help qualifying candidates — which is the opposite of what you are paying them for.

Questions to ask in the pitch

Beyond the domain fluency conversation, these are the questions worth asking any executive search firm you are evaluating:

  • Which specific senior AdTech, programmatic or advanced TV roles have you placed in the last twelve months?
  • Which of your clients are repeat-engaged, and what does that pattern tell us?
  • What is on your current off-limits list that would affect this search?
  • Who, by name, will personally run this mandate? What is their week-to-week involvement?
  • What is your structured competency-interview framework, and how does reference triangulation work?
  • What is your fee structure, and how is it staged?
  • What is your replacement guarantee, and what does it actually cover?

Summary

The right executive search firm for a senior AdTech, programmatic or advanced TV mandate in 2026 is almost always a boutique, founder-led firm with genuine operator-level domain fluency, a short and transparent off-limits list, and the honesty to match the engagement model to the brief. The wrong one is almost always a global generalist with a junior researcher tier doing the qualification work.
